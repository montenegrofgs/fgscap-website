Lessons Learned #06

Sales Drive the Company.

Every department either supports the sale—or makes it harder.

One of the most valuable lessons I have learned in business is simple: capital does not forgive indiscipline.

A company should become large without losing the cost discipline, urgency and customer focus it had when it was small. Increase fixed costs only when the business truly demands it. Maintain conservative leverage. Protect cash. And never forget who pays every salary, invoice and return: the customer.

There is an important difference between managing a company and owning one. Executives manage corporate resources. Owners feel every dollar leaving their pockets. The strongest executives learn to think like owners.

That is why I believe every company should be sales-driven—not only its sales department.

  • Legal sells when it protects the company without unnecessarily obstructing the client.
  • Marketing sells when it generates qualified demand, not merely visibility.
  • Development and design sell when they create the right product for the market.
  • Finance sells when it protects liquidity and preserves the ability to deliver.
  • Project controls sell when they identify risks before those risks become delays and losses.
  • Operations sell when they fulfill what the company promised.

Everyone influences the customer experience. Everyone either protects or consumes capital.

A sales-driven culture does not mean accepting every condition or selling at any price. It means understanding the difference between protecting margin and creating unnecessary friction.

I have seen partners believe they were negotiating well when they were actually making the purchase so difficult that the customer moved to a smarter competitor. Sometimes a fast “yes” simply means the company created the right product, at the right price, for the right client.

This matters especially in real estate development, where the financial cycle can last 12 to 36 months. Every sales delay increases carrying costs, financing expense and pressure on liquidity.

Build with discipline. Operate with an owner’s mindset. Make the entire company responsible for the customer.
