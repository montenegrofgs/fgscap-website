Lessons Learned #02

The $30 Million MBA of My Life.

What three decades of real projects taught me about P&L, debt, liquidity, governance and lasting value.

Early in my career, I had a conversation with a former global President of McDonald’s.

After hearing my investment thesis, he told me something I didn’t particularly enjoy hearing:

“Your investment thesis is good. But you don’t know how to read a P&L or a balance sheet.”

I was young. I didn’t pay much attention at the time. But I never forgot it.

So I decided to learn finance — not as an accountant, but as an owner, developer and executive.

I started studying the P&Ls and balance sheets of every public real estate and construction company I could access, in Brazil and abroad. And I discovered something fascinating: great companies tend to share similar financial disciplines.

  • They control costs before celebrating revenue.
  • They understand the right SG&A structure for their gross margin.
  • They watch debt, leverage and liquidity.
  • They know how long the company can survive if sales slow down.
  • They understand that profit and value are not the same thing.

Since then, whenever I analyze a development, construction company or troubled project, I start with the numbers: P&L. Balance sheet. Debt. Cash. Margins. Commitments. Only then do I diagnose the specific problem.

It is similar to medicine: examine the patient before prescribing the treatment.

It took me more than 30 years and exposure to more than $30 million in real decisions, investments, successes and expensive lessons to build this perspective.

It was the best MBA of my life. Not because I lost $30 million. Because I invested, built, made money, made mistakes, corrected course — and learned with my own capital and time at stake.

Those lessons helped shape what I do today through FGS Capital and our technical collaboration with CTE:

  • Development & Capital Structuring
  • Independent Engineer & Owner’s Representation
  • Development Advisory & Project Controls
  • Sustainability & Performance

Our objective is not simply to help a project generate profit. It is to help build lasting value.

Whether you are a small builder, a growing developer, a family office or an institutional investor, the principles are remarkably similar.

You don’t need to spend 30 years — or $30 million — learning them. I already did.

Let’s build value before risk becomes expensive.
