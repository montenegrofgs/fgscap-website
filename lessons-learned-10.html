Lessons Learned #10

Your P&L May Be Telling the Truth—and Still Hiding the Problem.

A project can show a profit on paper and still run out of cash.

The P&L may be correct. But it primarily explains what has already been recognized—not every obligation the project must still fund.

Real estate managers and investors also need visibility into:

  • available cash and restricted cash;
  • committed costs versus actual costs;
  • contracts not yet executed;
  • change orders and pending claims;
  • cost to complete;
  • loan availability and future draws;
  • sales, closings and collections;
  • upcoming payments and liquidity requirements.

Revenue is not always cash received.

Profit is not always liquidity.

And a construction budget is not complete simply because every invoice has not yet arrived.

This is where projects get into trouble. The financial statements may look acceptable while commitments, delayed collections and future construction costs quietly consume the remaining cash.

By the time the problem appears clearly in the P&L, management may have fewer options and much less negotiating power.

That is why project controls must connect accounting, construction, sales, financing and cash flow in one forward-looking view.

The most important number is not only what the project has spent.

It is: What will it cost to finish—and will the cash be available when needed?

Investors do not need more reports. They need reliable information that allows them to act before risk becomes a loss.
